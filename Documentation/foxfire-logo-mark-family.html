FPO
running fox
1 · The running foxprimary mark

The signature. Carries the brand's motion and equity; owns every context wider than tall. Its stacked lockup — mark over FOXFIRE over descriptor — is the primary lockup and the default answer to "which logo."

Formats: hero headers, banners, covers, shirts, print at size · Lockups: stacked (primary), mark-alone where the name is established · Floor: descriptor drops below ~160px lockup width; hands square and tiny duty to ranks 2–3

FPO
sitting fox
2 · The sitting foxcompact mark

The answer to aspect ratio. Circular-bodied where the runner is 2.35:1 wide, it owns square crops, tight placements, and the horizontal lockup the runner physically cannot make. Not the runner's rival — its complement.

Formats: horizontal lockup (nav bars, letterhead, email signatures), square and portrait placements, avatars ≥ ~48px · Lockups: horizontal (secondary), stacked compact, mark-alone

FPO
silhouette
3 · The silhouetteglyph

The sitting pose reduced to one solid shape — the same animal at its smallest density, per the two-weight doctrine. Where line dies and the coin scrapes by, the silhouette reads.

Formats: favicon, app icon small slots, embroidery, stamps · Range: ~16–40px, threshold to be pinned against rank 2's floor · self-grounded coin treatment optional for tab-bar duty

Which one do I grab

Wider than tall? Running fox — stacked lockup if the name needs saying, mark alone if it doesn't.

Square, circular, or a wide-short strip? Sitting fox — horizontal lockup for strips, mark alone for crops and avatars.

Tiny? The silhouette. Below ~40px nothing else survives, and nothing else is allowed to try.

States

Every family member inherits the four states — full color on light, dark-state inks on dark (#0A7571 body, Golden Hour 4 descriptor), Starlight line-positive knockout, and reduction. States are orthogonal to rank: one rule set, three marks.
